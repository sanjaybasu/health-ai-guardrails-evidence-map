Methods

Question

For each practice that governance documents recommend for deploying artificial intelligence in health care, including large language models, multimodal and foundation models, predictive risk models, imaging and diagnostic software, ambient documentation, and AI agents, we ask how many sources endorse it, whether any study has tested it, how strong the strongest test is, and whether any test included Medicaid, safety-net, limited English proficiency, or low-literacy populations. The design follows interactive evidence tools that attach the evidence to each option a decision-maker is choosing among, such as the statin ranking platform of Hopkin and colleagues (Med Decis Making 2019; doi:10.1177/0272989X19835239).

Recommendations and endorsements

We fetched the full text of {{ s.n_sources }} governance sources (frameworks, accreditation guidance, federal regulation, state statutes, and peer-reviewed commentaries; listed in governance_sources.yaml). Three models from three vendors (Anthropic Claude Opus 5.5, OpenAI GPT-6, Google Gemini 3.1 Pro) each extracted every atomic, checkable deployment recommendation with a verbatim supporting quote and tagged the kinds of AI it concerns. We kept a candidate only if its quote appears in the source text after whitespace and punctuation normalization. Candidates were embedded, clustered by cosine similarity, and merged into canonical recommendations by one model; the other two vendors critiqued the merged list for bundled, duplicate, untestable, or overreaching items, and the merging model revised it. Endorsements are counted as distinct sources; commentaries retrieved from the literature add endorsements only when the abstract states the recommendation verbatim. Endorsements are never counted as evidence.

Literature

Each week we run two queries, one for large language models and generative AI and one for deployment, validation, monitoring, and governance of any health care AI, against Europe PMC, which indexes PubMed and MEDLINE together with medRxiv, bioRxiv, Research Square, and other preprint servers, and arXiv, restricting terms to titles and abstracts. OpenAlex supplies citation counts. Bibliographic details come only from these services, never from model output. The corpus holds {{ "{:,}".format(s.n_papers) }} records, of which {{ "{:,}".format(s.n_preprints) }} are preprints.

Screening and extraction

A low-cost model screens each title and abstract into empirical studies, recommendation-bearing commentaries, reviews, and out-of-scope records, and flags studies that test a deployment practice. A second vendor re-screens a random {{ "5%" }} sample of exclusions{% if s.screen_audit %}; in the latest audit it disagreed on {{ s.screen_audit.disagreements }} of {{ s.screen_audit.sampled }} sampled exclusions{% endif %}. Studies that test a practice go to the three-vendor panel, which reads the abstract and, when open access, the methods and results. A link between a study and a recommendation is kept when at least two of three vendors make it and each supporting quote is verbatim; categorical fields are resolved by majority vote, and the site reports per-field agreement. Studies that only document a hazard, and commentaries, are extracted by a single model with the same quote check.

Two kinds of evidence

A test compares outcomes with and without a practice, or measures whether the practice achieves its aim (for example, whether clinician review catches errors in AI-drafted messages). A hazard study measures the failure a practice targets (for example, the reading level of LLM answers to patient questions) without testing the practice. Most published LLM studies are hazard studies. They show that a problem exists; they do not show that a given guardrail solves it.

Evidence levels

LevelDesign
1Randomized trial of the practice in a real deployment
2Controlled comparison in a real deployment (quasi-experimental or prospective with a comparator)
3Deployment data without a comparator (pre-post, descriptive, qualitative)
4Simulation, vignette, or retrospective replay with human raters
5Automated benchmark only

Levels describe design and setting; they are not a full risk-of-bias assessment. A recommendation is labelled supported only when its strongest test is level 1 or 2, that test favours the practice, and no test at the same or a stronger level found against it.

Limitations

Every link is machine-extracted until a person verifies it, and the site marks verification status on each item. Paywalled full text is not read, so some tests reported only in full text are missed. The taxonomy began as a machine draft and is curated by hand; the weekly update proposes new recommendations but adds them only after human review. Non-English literature is not searched.

Updates

A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow runs every Monday, adds new records, re-fetches governance sources to detect revisions, rebuilds this site, and opens a pull request summarizing the changes for human review before they are published.

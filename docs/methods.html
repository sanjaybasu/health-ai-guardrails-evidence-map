Methods

Question

For each practice that governance documents recommend for deploying artificial intelligence in health care, including large language models, multimodal and foundation models, predictive risk models, imaging and diagnostic software, ambient documentation, and AI agents, we ask how many sources endorse it, whether any study has tested it, how strong the strongest test is, and whether any test included Medicaid, safety-net, limited English proficiency, or low-literacy populations. The design follows interactive evidence tools that attach the evidence to each option a decision-maker is choosing among, such as the statin ranking platform of Hopkin and colleagues (Med Decis Making 2019; doi:10.1177/0272989X19835239).

Recommendations and endorsements

We fetched the full text of 19 governance sources (frameworks, accreditation guidance, federal regulation, state statutes, and peer-reviewed commentaries; listed in governance_sources.yaml). Three models from three vendors (Anthropic claude-opus-5-5, OpenAI gpt-6-astra, Google gemini-3.1-pro-preview) each extracted every atomic, checkable deployment recommendation with a verbatim supporting quote and tagged the kinds of AI it concerns. We kept a candidate only if its quote appears in the source text after whitespace and punctuation normalization. Candidates were embedded, clustered by cosine similarity, and merged into canonical recommendations by one model; the other two vendors critiqued the merged list for bundled, duplicate, untestable, or overreaching items, and the merging model revised it. Endorsements are counted as distinct sources; commentaries retrieved from the literature add endorsements only when the abstract states the recommendation verbatim. Endorsements are never counted as evidence.

Journal editorials

Because many editorials, perspectives, and viewpoints carry no abstract, keyword searches miss them. We therefore pull every AI-titled item since 2023 from 16 journals through OpenAlex (NEJM AI, the New England Journal of Medicine, NEJM Catalyst, the Lancet, Lancet Digital Health, JAMA, JAMA Internal Medicine, JAMA Network Open, JAMA Health Forum, Nature Medicine, npj Digital Medicine, BMJ, Annals of Internal Medicine, Health Affairs, JAMIA, and Radiology: Artificial Intelligence) and screen them with the rest of the corpus. Their recommendations appear as a separate source type, never as evidence. We read only legally open full text, so many paywalled editorials could not be read; the table gives coverage by journal.

Literature

Each week we run two queries, one for large language models and generative AI and one for deployment, validation, monitoring, and governance of any health care AI, against Europe PMC, which indexes PubMed and MEDLINE together with medRxiv, bioRxiv, Research Square, and other preprint servers, and arXiv, restricting terms to titles and abstracts. OpenAlex supplies citation counts. Bibliographic details come only from these services, never from model output. The corpus holds 40,053 records, of which 6,806 are preprints.

Screening and extraction

A low-cost model (gemini-3.8-flash) screens each title and abstract into empirical studies, recommendation-bearing commentaries, reviews, and out-of-scope records, tags the kinds of AI involved, and flags studies that test a deployment practice. A calibrated classifier (Jev, TypeSafe) independently estimates the probability that each record tests a practice and that the test took place in a real deployment; records on which the two disagree, or on which Jev's probability lies between 0.2 and 0.8, are re-screened by a third model (claude-sonnet-5-5), whose decision is used. A second vendor also re-screens a random 5% sample of exclusions each month; in the latest audit it disagreed on 26 of 200 sampled exclusions.

Studies that test a practice in a real deployment go to a panel of three frontier models from three vendors (claude-opus-5-5, gpt-6-astra, gemini-3.1-pro-preview); studies that test a practice offline or in simulation go to a panel of three faster models from the same three vendors (claude-sonnet-5-5, gpt-5.5, gemini-3.8-flash). Each panel reads the abstract and, when open access, the methods and results, and it sees every recommendation in the domains a routing model judged relevant to the study. A link between a study and a recommendation is kept when at least two of three vendors make it independently, or when one vendor makes it and at least one other vendor, shown the study and the proposed link, agrees. Every supporting quote must appear verbatim in the text. Categorical fields are resolved by majority vote, and the site reports per-field agreement. Studies that only document a hazard are extracted by a single model; commentaries are extracted from open-access full text only, because abstracts rarely state a recommendation verbatim.

For every link, Jev also estimates the probability that the quoted sentence reports a result bearing on the linked recommendation. The site shows this as quote support, and links below 50% are queued first for human review. Human review determines whether a link is marked verified.

Across 1485 panel-extracted studies, mean three-vendor agreement was design 0.760; setting 0.934; user 0.977; country 0.724; sample unit 0.601; sample size 0.918; population 0.992.

Two kinds of evidence

A test compares outcomes with and without a practice, or measures whether the practice achieves its aim (for example, whether clinician review catches errors in AI-drafted messages). A hazard study measures the failure a practice targets (for example, the reading level of LLM answers to patient questions) without testing the practice. Most published LLM studies are hazard studies. They show that a problem exists; they do not show that a given guardrail solves it.

Evidence levels

LevelDesign
1Randomized trial of the practice in a real deployment
2Controlled comparison in a real deployment (quasi-experimental or prospective with a comparator)
3Deployment data without a comparator (pre-post, descriptive, qualitative)
4Simulation, vignette, or retrospective replay with human raters
5Automated benchmark only

Levels describe design and setting; they are not a full risk-of-bias assessment. A recommendation is labelled supported only when its strongest test is level 1 or 2, that test favours the practice, and no test at the same or a stronger level found against it.

Technical controls

Governance documents rarely address implementation choices such as filtering model output with keywords or a classifier, grounding answers in retrieved sources, verifying one model with another, abstaining below a confidence threshold, or translating inputs before processing. The technical-controls view is built from the bottom up: during extraction, each study and commentary also recorded practices it tested or recommended that match no governance recommendation. A low-cost model sorted those practices into 14 families of implementation choices; the merging model grouped them into decisions stated with an explicit comparator; and models from the two other vendors critiqued the list before revision. Every study that tests a practice is then linked to the decisions it compared, with the same rules as the main map: all vendors see the same candidate list, a link needs agreement from at least two of three vendors, and the supporting quote must appear verbatim. For each link the site records the comparator the study used, which arm the result favoured, and any tradeoff the authors reported, such as cost, latency, or new failure modes. Most comparisons of implementation choices come from simulations and benchmarks, and the site labels each by evidence level.

Filtering by technology

Every recommendation and every study carries technology tags (large language models and generative AI, multimodal and foundation models, predictive risk models, imaging and diagnostic software, ambient documentation, AI agents, and simulation tools). When a technology is selected, a recommendation's status, strongest evidence level, and counts are recomputed from studies of that technology alone, so a practice supported by imaging trials is not shown as supported for language models. General recommendations stay visible, marked untested for that technology when no study of it exists; the checkbox beside the filter limits the list to recommendations written for that technology.

Limitations

Every link is machine-extracted until a person verifies it, and the site marks verification status on each item. Paywalled full text is not read, so some tests reported only in full text are missed. The taxonomy began as a machine draft and is curated by hand; the weekly update proposes new recommendations but adds them only after human review. Non-English literature is not searched.

Updates

A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow runs every Monday, adds new records, re-fetches governance sources to detect revisions, rebuilds this site, and opens a pull request summarizing the changes. The pull request is merged and published automatically when the test suite passes and a validation check confirms that every page and data file loads and that no recommendations or studies were lost; otherwise it stays open for manual review. New links appear on the site marked unverified until a person reviews them.
